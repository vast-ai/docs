Vast.ai lets hosts attach a **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** to a machine listing. An SLA is a reliability guarantee: clients who choose an SLA pay a small hourly **SLA fee** included in **`dph_total`** (dollars per hour total in search — GPU + storage + SLA fee), and if measured reliability falls short of their chosen target, they receive a service credit funded from your earnings.

The **SLA fee** is that hourly amount. It appears as the **SLA Fee** line in the console. API field names call the same amount a premium (for example `slaPremiumPerHour`).

**New to SLA?** Read [SLA in plain language](#sla-in-plain-language) first, then return here for economics and API detail.

This page explains how SLA offers work from the host side — what you set, how clients choose an SLA, and what happens when uptime is good or bad.

<Note title="For hosts">
SLA settlement is **asymmetric by design**: on strong days you keep the SLA fee; on bad days, service credits can exceed the fees you collected. Charges and credits settle on a daily cadence. Preview economics with the [SLA backtester](/host/sla-backtester) (API, requires rental history) or the web console SLA preview (**Set Pricing → SLA**).
</Note>

<Note title="This page is for hosts">
**Clients:** see [SLA](/guides/instances/choosing/sla-coverage) for how SLA appears in search and billing.
</Note>

## SLA host checklist

1. Complete [Account setup and hosting agreement](/host/hosting-overview#account-setup-and-hosting-agreement) on a **dedicated host account**, then [host setup](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/setup/). Confirm the machine appears on **Host → Machines** (healthy; GPU price and offer end date saved under **Set Pricing**—listing not required yet).
2. Create and fund a separate **client** account for later self-test (see [Testing your own machine](/host/hosting-overview#testing-your-own-machine) account setup)—do **not** rent your machine yet; listing comes in step 8.
3. Install the [CLI](/cli/hello-world) if you schedule maintenance or read earnings from the CLI. The console [Set a maintenance window](/host/set-maintenance-window) page can notify renters without the CLI.
4. Confirm SLA account access: open **Set Pricing → SLA** and confirm the panel is visible (do **not** Save final commitment/confidence yet). If the panel is missing, contact support.
5. Preview economics (console SLA panel or [backtester](/host/sla-backtester)). Read [Billing vs settlement](#billing-vs-settlement-important) and [Risk scenarios](#risk-scenarios)—GPU billing can continue during outages and SLA credits can exceed many days of SLA fees.
6. Set and **Save** your reliability commitment and confidence in **Set Pricing → SLA**.
7. Read [Scheduled maintenance](#scheduled-maintenance) before your first SLA rental (24-hour notice for planned work).
8. **First publish and verify:** **Save** pricing, then click **LIST** (first publish only). If the machine is already listed, **Save** alone updates the live offer.
   - **Console verify:** confirm **Listed** on **Host → Machines** and reopen **Set Pricing → SLA**.
   - **Optional CLI/API:** search with `--storage` matching how renters quote; add `verified=any` if you search your own machine and see no rows (for example `vastai search offers 'machine_id=12345 verified=any has_sla=true expected_reliability>=0.99' --storage 10`). On sliced machines, filter on-demand rows with the jq example in [Search / client-visible fields](#search--client-visible-fields).
9. **Self-test (after LIST):** With the separate client account from step 2, search by `machine_id`, rent with a **Reliability target**, and verify the SLA (billed like any rental)—see [Testing your own machine](/host/hosting-overview#testing-your-own-machine).

For maintenance forgiveness, follow [Scheduled maintenance](#scheduled-maintenance): at least 24 hours' notice. Forgiveness is not guaranteed, and it is not a service credit paid to you.
</Warning>

- Ensure your host daemon is current per the [host setup guide](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/setup/).
- If listing fails with `sla_not_approved` or `sla_daemon_outdated`, resolve eligibility before changing SLA parameters.
- **Planned maintenance tooling:** Use [`vastai schedule maint`](/host/cli/schedule-maint) or REST — see [Scheduled maintenance](#scheduled-maintenance).

Complete [host setup](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/setup/) first. A healthy machine appears on **Host → Machines** with no red error status.

Before enabling SLA, finish normal listing setup: test your machine, set GPU price and offer end date, choose on-demand vs interruptible, and understand [Testing your own machine](/host/hosting-overview#testing-your-own-machine) and offer terms in [Hosting overview](/host/hosting-overview).

<Warning>
On outage days you may still bill clients for **intended** compute **and** owe service credits. Tail-risk exposure can approach a full day’s compute per affected contract — not just the daily SLA fee shown in previews. Read [Billing vs settlement](#billing-vs-settlement-important) before enabling SLA.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Rental types:** The SLA applies to **on-demand rentals only**. It does not apply to interruptible/bid instances or reserved/prepay rentals. Interruptible rentals are not covered by the SLA, even on machines that offer an SLA for on-demand rentals. On-demand contracts on the same machine can have an SLA. Reserved or prepay attempts on an offer with an SLA are not supported — use on-demand to rent with an SLA.
</Warning>

- Optional: run the [backtester](/host/sla-backtester) after you have weeks of history; otherwise use the web console SLA preview and the economics walkthrough on this page.
- **New or low-history machines:** If the backtester returns `has_data: false`, rely on the web console SLA preview. Wait for rental history before trusting backtester results. Consider wider confidence or a more conservative reliability commitment until you have a few weeks of data.

## SLA in plain language

SLA is an optional reliability promise on **on-demand** rentals. You do not have to offer it.

1. **You (the host)** state a **reliability commitment**: how often you expect the machine to be up. The API field is `sla_r_claim`. The console may still label this **Claim**.
2. You also set **confidence**: how sure you are of that commitment. This is a spread, not odds. Tighter confidence makes the renter's SLA fee smaller.
3. The **renter** picks a **reliability target**. You do not set their target.
4. The renter pays an **SLA fee** (a service level fee) on top of the GPU price.
5. Each day, Vast checks how long that rental was actually up.
6. If the blended measure met their reliability target, you keep the SLA fee. "Met" uses Vast's blended reliability measure (recent and longer-term uptime), not that day's raw uptime alone. A day whose raw uptime is at or slightly above the target can still produce a partial credit if the blended measure falls below it. When the blended measure meets the target, no credit is paid.
7. If uptime missed their reliability target, the renter receives a **service credit** from your earnings. One bad day can cost more than many days of fees.

What this is not:

- It is not the reliability score on the offer card.
- Listing the machine does not turn SLA on. You turn it on in **Set Pricing → SLA** (or with the listing API). The CLI `list machine` command cannot set it today.
- It applies only to **on-demand** rentals, not interruptible or reserved ones.
- An outage does not erase that day's GPU bill. The renter's compensation is a separate credit.

The field names for the reliability commitment (`sla_r_claim`), confidence, and reliability target are in [How it works (plain English)](#how-it-works-plain-english) below. Renters get the same story in [SLA in plain language](/guides/instances/choosing/sla-coverage#sla-in-plain-language).

## How it works (plain English)

<Note title="Console hosts — read this first">
In **Host → Machines → Set Pricing → SLA** you set host knobs only:

- **Reliability commitment** — how often you expect the machine to be up (shown to clients as `expected_reliability`; API field `sla_r_claim`; the console may still label this Claim).
- **Confidence** — how tightly you price around that commitment (`sla_sigma_x`; lower = tighter / cheaper for clients at a given reliability target). This is not odds.

Clients choose their own **reliability target** at search and rent time. You do **not** set the client target on the listing.
</Note>

Think of an SLA offer as two host ideas plus one client choice:

1. **Your reliability commitment** — what you believe your machine will deliver (`sla_r_claim`). Setting this above `0` enables SLA on the offer.
2. **Your confidence** — how wide your belief is around that reliability commitment (`sla_sigma_x`). Tighter confidence → lower SLA fees for clients at a given target; wider confidence → higher charges.
3. **Client reliability target** — chosen by the renter via search (`target_reliability`). Settlement and SLA fee sizing use this target. A renter's target must be **at or below** your reliability commitment. Search hides offers whose commitment is below the target, and rent rejects that combination.

When a client rents an on-demand instance on your SLA-listed offer with a chosen target:

- While a rental **contract** is active, clients pay GPU, storage, bandwidth (usage-based), and a **small hourly SLA fee** — including during downtime. Settlement credits for missed targets are calculated separately at UTC day boundaries. In search results, **`dph_total`** is the quoted hourly rental cost: GPU + storage + SLA fee at the quoted allocation — **not** bandwidth.
- Each UTC calendar day, Vast measures reliability for the contract and settles the SLA.
- **Strong reliability relative to the client's target** → you keep the SLA fee for that day.
- **Shortfalls** → credits flow to the client from your earnings (bounded by that day's covered compute).

Over many strong days, SLA fees can offset occasional credits, but a single bad day can exceed months of collected charges — see [Risk scenarios](#risk-scenarios) and the backtester's [largest single-day credit](/host/sla-backtester#summarize-with-jq) before you commit.

### How reliability is measured

SLA reliability is measured at the **rental contract (instance) level** using platform health signals for active on-demand contracts — not your personal SSH session or Jupyter tab.

- **Instance fails to start** when a client rents → counts against SLA reliability for that contract.
- **Machine-level outage** while contracts are active → affects settlement for those contracts.
- **Brief host reboots** during active rentals may count as downtime. Vast does not publish a fixed second-level threshold in host docs — treat unplanned reboots as SLA risk. For planned work, use [Scheduled maintenance](#scheduled-maintenance) with at least 24 hours' notice when possible.
- **Scheduled maintenance** may be partly forgiven (counted as uptime) — see [Scheduled maintenance](#scheduled-maintenance). That is not a service credit.

On sliced machines, SLA settles **per rental contract**. If one GPU slice fails but others remain healthy, only contracts on the affected slice are measured for downtime.

### Billing vs settlement (important)

For SLA-covered on-demand contracts, compute billing uses the **intended daily compute** for the rental. Offline time is tracked as an outage in reliability observations. Client compensation for poor reliability flows through **service credits**, not by silently zeroing the day's compute bill.

## SLA parameters

When you list or update a machine, set SLA fields via the host console or `PUT /api/v0/machines/create_asks/` (see [Listing an SLA offer](#listing-an-sla-offer) below).

| Parameter | API field | Backtester alias | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability commitment | `sla_r_claim` | `r_claim` (the simulated renter target `r_target` defaults to it) | Your advertised belief about machine reliability. Set above `0` to enable SLA. Values are greater than 0 and at most **0.99999** (for example, `0.99` = 99%). Exactly `1.0` is rejected. Set to `0` to disable SLA on new rentals. |
| Confidence spread | `sla_sigma_x` | `p` (different scale — see [backtester](/host/sla-backtester#confidence-values-p)) | How wide your belief is around the reliability commitment. Lower = more confident (lower SLA fees for clients at a given target). Defaults to `0.5` if omitted. |

### Advanced: `sla_max_beta` (optional)

**Support-only — omit unless Vast support directs you.** Hosts do **not** set β (timescale mix). The platform derives β from the client's `target_reliability` at rent time.

`sla_max_beta` is an optional ask term that caps how aggressively long-running contracts may weigh recent uptime versus longer history in settlement. Leave it unset for an uncapped offer. If you set it, it must be in `(0, 1)` and at least the default β derived at your reliability commitment — otherwise listing is rejected.

<Warning>
**`sla_r_target` is not host-settable.** If you send `sla_r_target` or legacy `sla_reliability` on `create_asks/`, they are **ignored**. Clients supply their reliability target at search/rent time (`target_reliability`). Do **not** copy numeric `p` values from the SLA Backtester into `sla_sigma_x` — they use different scales. Use the mapping table on the [backtester page](/host/sla-backtester#confidence-values-p).
</Warning>

<Note title="Terminology">
| Concept | Console | Listing API | Backtester | Client sees / sets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability commitment | Claim | `sla_r_claim` | `r_claim` | `expected_reliability` |
| Confidence | Confidence / spread | `sla_sigma_x` | `p` (different scale) | — |
| Reliability target | — (client-chosen) | — (not on asks) | `r_target` (optional; defaults to `r_claim`) | `target_reliability` in search |
| All-in hourly price | — | — | — | `dph_total` (compute plus SLA fee when enabled) |

**Confidence is not an uptime target.** It controls how aggressively SLA fees are priced around your reliability commitment.
</Note>

### Machine reliability score vs SLA reliability

The **reliability score** on the Host Machines page reflects connection and instance-start behavior over recent activity. **SLA settlement** measures whether covered instances met the renter's chosen reliability target for billing purposes.

Do not assume the dashboard score equals SLA reliability. Before choosing `sla_r_claim`:

1. Open **Host → Machines** → **Set Pricing → SLA** and review the console SLA fee preview at candidate reliability commitments.
2. If you have rental history, run the [backtester](/host/sla-backtester) and compare `r_eff` (effective reliability) with `r_be` (break-even reliability) in the response.
3. Set **reliability commitment** at a level you can sustain; clients who pick targets below your reliability commitment see lower SLA fees.

### Confidence values

**This only changes how SLA fees are priced — it does not change how reliability is measured or what credit you owe when a client's target is missed.** Prefer the console controls. Set raw `sla_sigma_x` via the API when automating.

| Console label | API `sla_sigma_x` | Backtester `p` | Pricing effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tight | `0.2` | ~0.83 | Lowest client SLA fees at a given target |
| Normal (default) | `0.5` | ~0.6667 | Balanced |
| Wide | `1.0` | ~0.50 | Highest client SLA fees at a given target |

**Never copy backtester `p` into `sla_sigma_x` — the scales are inverted.** See the full mapping on the [backtester page](/host/sla-backtester#confidence-values-p).

**Tighter confidence** (tighter spread; console label **Tight**) → lower client SLA fees (more competitive `dph_total`). **Wider confidence** (console label **Wide**) → higher client SLA fees. Confidence does **not** change the credit formula when a target is missed — use the backtester's [largest single-day credit](/host/sla-backtester#summarize-with-jq) and `r_target` what-ifs for tail-risk planning.

### Validation rules

Offers with invalid combinations are rejected at listing time:

- `sla_r_claim` must be `0` (disable) or greater than 0 and at most **0.99999**. Exactly `1.0` is rejected.
- When the reliability commitment is positive, `sla_sigma_x` must be positive and at most `3.0` (defaults to `0.5` if omitted).
- Optional `sla_max_beta` must be in `(0, 1)` and at least the default β derived at your reliability commitment. Hosts do not set β; this field only caps the client-derived β the offer accepts. Omit for uncapped.

<Note>
If listing is rejected with `error: "invalid_sla_params"`, check the `msg` field against the rules above. Other SLA listing errors include `sla_not_approved` and `sla_daemon_outdated`.
</Note>

### SLA and GPU slicing

SLA settlement applies per rental contract. If your machine has multiple GPU slices rented by different clients (GPU slicing — see **Min GPU** in [Hosting Overview](/host/hosting-overview)), each contract's SLA is settled independently based on the reliability experienced by that instance.

The backtester replays **machine-level** daily earnings (total compute across all active rentals on that machine). Its largest single-day credit reflects the worst simulated day for the whole machine in the replay window — not a single GPU slice in isolation.

If multiple on-demand SLA contracts can be active on one machine at once, a single outage may trigger service credits **per contract**. The backtester’s largest single-day credit reflects a machine-level replay as one contract—it may not equal the sum of worst-case credits across concurrent SLA renters. For sliced hosts, treat it as a **lower bound** until you confirm aggregation with support. Do **not** naively multiply it by contract count as a substitute for that check—the backtester already models machine-level compute for the replay window, but live per-contract settlement can still stack differently than one simulated day.

### SLA on extended contracts

When a rental contract is extended (by moving the offer end date forward at the same or lower price), the original SLA parameters remain in effect for the extended period. Changing SLA parameters on the offer does not alter SLA terms on existing contracts — only new rental contracts pick up updated SLA settings.

Disabling SLA on the offer (setting `sla_r_claim` to `0`) prevents new SLA-covered contracts but does not terminate SLA on existing active contracts — they continue under their original SLA terms until their rental end date.

## Listing an SLA offer

**Console hosts:** use [Web console](#web-console) and, after you list, [Console (recommended for day-to-day checks)](#console-recommended-for-day-to-day-checks). [API (for automation)](#api-for-automation) and [Fleet SLA re-list](#fleet-sla-re-list-machines-that-already-have-an-sla) are for API keys.

### Web console

1. Open your machine on the [Host Machines page](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/machines) → **Set Pricing**.
2. Set base listing fields first (GPU price, offer end date, rental types) if this is a new listing.
3. Expand **SLA**.
4. Set your **reliability commitment** (the console control is **Claim**; the API field is `sla_r_claim`). Renters see it as expected reliability on the offer. Set **Confidence** too. For your first listing, choose **Normal** (`sla_sigma_x ≈ 0.5`) unless your uptime history is volatile — see [Confidence values](#confidence-values) for risk tradeoffs.
5. Save and confirm the SLA fee preview before listing.
6. After saving pricing, click **LIST** on the Host Machines page (or confirm the machine status changes to **listed**). Saving pricing alone stores your offer settings; listing publishes the offer to search.

#### Updating SLA on an already-listed machine

If the machine is already **listed**:

1. Open **Host → Machines → Set Pricing → SLA**, set Claim and Confidence, then click **Save**.
2. **Save** applies SLA to the live offer — you do not need to click **LIST** again unless this is a brand-new listing that was never published.
3. Verify after listing:
   - **Console:** confirm the machine status is **Listed**, reopen **Set Pricing → SLA** to review the preview, or check offer details under **Host → Machines**.
   - **Search:** `dph_total` includes the SLA fee at a client's `target_reliability`, and `expected_reliability` matches your reliability commitment (see [Search / client-visible fields](#search--client-visible-fields)).

<Note title="Console vs API">
In the web console, **Set Pricing → Save** stores SLA settings; clicking **LIST** publishes the offer to search. `PUT /api/v0/machines/create_asks/` performs both steps in one request — it creates or updates the **live listing** (same publish step as **LIST**; SLA parameters require this REST body or **Set Pricing → SLA**, not `vastai list machine` flags).
</Note>

#### Web console SLA preview

In the host console, open **Host Machines** → select your machine → **Set Pricing** → **SLA**. The preview shows estimated SLA fee and service-credit exposure for the parameters you enter. It is a forward-looking quote, not a historical replay — use the [SLA Backtester](/host/sla-backtester) for history-based simulation.

### API (for automation)

New hosts: use the web console steps above first. Use the API when you need scripting, partial updates, or fleet automation.

<Note>
SLA listing via CLI/SDK is not available yet — use the REST API below (or console **Set Pricing → SLA**). See also [List Machine](/api-reference/machines/list-machine) for the same `create_asks` body.
</Note>

#### Before you start

1. Open **Account → API Keys** and create a key. See [Create API Key](/api-reference/accounts/create-api-key).
2. For listing SLA offers, enable **`machine_write`**. For backtesting first, add **`machine_read`**. For post-listing earnings, add **`billing_read`**.
3. Use the key as `Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_KEY` in the `curl` examples below. See [Authentication](/api-reference/authentication).
4. To enable SLA today, use `PUT /api/v0/machines/create_asks/` or **Set Pricing → SLA** in the console. The listing body field is `machine` (same ID as backtester `machine_id` and earnings `machid`).
5. For the full request/response schema, see [List Machine](/api-reference/machines/list-machine).

#### Update SLA on an existing ask (partial body)

<Warning>
**Not a first listing.** Use this SLA-only body when the machine already has base pricing. For a **first publish**, use a full `create_asks` body from [List Machine](/api-reference/machines/list-machine) (include at least `min_chunk`, disk/bandwidth prices, and `end_date`) or **Set Pricing → SLA** in the console. See [Hosting overview](/host/hosting-overview#offers-and-rental-contracts).
</Warning>

```bash
curl -s -X PUT "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/machines/create_asks/" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"machine": 12345, "price_disk": 0.15, "hidden_listing": false, "end_date": "CURRENT_END_DATE_FROM_SHOW_MACHINES", "sla_r_claim": 0.99, "sla_sigma_x": 0.5}'
```

Replace `price_disk` with the machine's current `listed_storage_cost`. Replace `end_date` with the machine's **current** `end_date` from `show machines` (a Unix timestamp, not the placeholder string). Send `duration` as an integer number of seconds if the listing auto-renews (`show machines` returns a float such as `2592000.0`, which the API rejects), otherwise send `end_date`. Never send both. Send `"hidden_listing": true` if the listing is hidden. See the warning below. Do not copy example prices into a first listing.

<Warning>
Send your **current** `end_date` unless you intend to extend. A later `end_date` at the same or lower price extends existing rental contracts, including their SLA terms and service-credit exposure. See [Extending rental contracts](/host/hosting-overview#extending-rental-contracts).
</Warning>

#### Full listing update (existing ask)

When you also need to refresh GPU price or `end_date` on an already-listed machine:

```bash
curl -s -X PUT "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/machines/create_asks/" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "machine": 12345,
    "price_gpu": 3.50,
    "price_disk": 0.15,
    "hidden_listing": false,
    "sla_r_claim": 0.99,
    "sla_sigma_x": 0.5,
    "end_date": "CURRENT_END_DATE_FROM_SHOW_MACHINES"
  }'
```

Replace `end_date` with the listing's current Unix timestamp from `show machines`, or with a new future timestamp only if you mean to change the offer end. Past dates expire the offer immediately. A later date at the same or lower price extends live contracts. See [Offer End Date](/host/hosting-overview#offer-end-date). Convert a calendar date to Unix seconds: `date -u -d '2030-01-01' +%s` (Linux) or `date -u -j -f '%Y-%m-%d' '2030-01-01' +%s` (macOS). The CLI also accepts `MM/DD/YYYY` via `-e`.

<Warning>Open-ended offers (no `end_date`) combined with SLA expose you to indefinite asymmetric service-credit risk. See [Offer End Date](/host/hosting-overview#offer-end-date).</Warning>

SLA fields example (existing listing, same limit as the curl above):

```json
{
  "machine": 12345,
  "price_gpu": 3.50,
  "price_disk": 0.15,
  "hidden_listing": false,
  "end_date": "CURRENT_END_DATE_FROM_SHOW_MACHINES",
  "sla_r_claim": 0.99,
  "sla_sigma_x": 0.5
}
```

This creates an offer with a reliability commitment of `0.99` and confidence spread `0.5`. Clients who search with `target_reliability` below your reliability commitment see a lower SLA fee than clients who choose a reliability target near your reliability commitment.

<Warning>
`create_asks` is not a pure partial update. If you change only SLA fields, the platform re-validates the merged SLA parameters. Set `sla_r_claim` to `0` to disable SLA on an existing listing. Other omitted fields are not all kept:

- `price_gpu` is kept if omitted.
- `price_disk` resets to **$0.10/GB/month** if omitted or `null`.
- `duration` is cleared if omitted (a listing created with `vastai list machine -l` stops auto-renewing).
- `hidden_listing` resets to `false` if omitted (a hidden listing becomes visible). `show machines` does not return this flag, so send `true` when the listing should stay hidden.
- Send **either** `duration` (when `show machines` returns a non-null `duration`) **or** `end_date`, never both. Both together returns `400` `Cannot set both duration and end_date`.

Always read the current listing and send `price_disk` plus either `duration` or `end_date`. Map read-side names as follows: `listed_gpu_cost` → `price_gpu`, `listed_storage_cost` → `price_disk`.
</Warning>

Read current listing fields from `vastai show machines --raw`:

```bash
vastai show machines --raw | jq '.machines[] | select(.id == 12345 or .id == "12345") | {id, listed, sla_reliability_claim, sla_confidence, listed_gpu_cost, listed_storage_cost, listed_min_gpu_count, end_date, duration}'
```

REST equivalent: `GET /api/v0/machines/` (the CLI sends `?owner=me`). The account is the API key owner; a `?user_id=` query is ignored.

Machine `id` may be a string in raw JSON. The response is wrapped in a `machines` array. Read-side names differ from the `create_asks` body: `listed_gpu_cost` → `price_gpu`, `listed_min_gpu_count` → `min_chunk`, `sla_reliability_claim` → `sla_r_claim`. Confidence is returned as a label, `sla_confidence` (`high` = Tight, `medium` = Normal, `low` = Wide), not as `sla_sigma_x`. `sla_reliability_claim` and `sla_confidence` are `null` unless **all** of these are true: SLA is enabled on the platform, the machine is listed, your account is SLA-approved, and the machine's daemon meets the SLA minimum version. A machine with saved SLA terms can still return `null` if it is unlisted, its daemon is outdated, or approval was revoked. A listed machine with SLA turned off returns `0`. Pricing fields and `end_date` are `null` when the machine is not listed.

Then `PUT /api/v0/machines/create_asks/` with the merged body (read → merge → PUT). `vastai list machine` and the Python SDK listing helpers cannot set SLA fields; start from this REST pattern (or the console) when you move an existing CLI listing to SLA.

### Fleet SLA re-list (machines that already have an SLA)

**Console hosts:** skip this section. Enable an SLA with [Web console](#web-console) and check settlement in [Console (recommended for day-to-day checks)](#console-recommended-for-day-to-day-checks).
</Note>

Use this loop to republish SLA terms across a fleet, for example after a daemon update. It does not turn an SLA on for new machines. Enable an SLA on each machine first (console **Set Pricing → SLA**, or a per-machine `PUT /api/v0/machines/create_asks/` with `sla_r_claim` and `sla_sigma_x`). The loop only considers machines that are `listed` and already have `sla_reliability_claim > 0`.

```bash

# Set DRY_RUN=0 to apply.

ALLOWLIST="${ALLOWLIST:-}"
# Hidden listings: show machines does not return hidden_listing, and omitting it
# makes the listing public. Put every hidden machine ID here, or leave it off ALLOWLIST.
HIDDEN_IDS="${HIDDEN_IDS:-}"
DRY_RUN="${DRY_RUN:-1}"
machines=$(vastai show machines --raw)
for id in $(echo "$machines" | jq -r '.machines[].id'); do
  if [ "$DRY_RUN" = "0" ] && [ -z "$ALLOWLIST" ]; then
    echo "abort: set ALLOWLIST to Listed machine IDs before DRY_RUN=0"; break
  fi
  if [ -n "$ALLOWLIST" ] && ! echo "$ALLOWLIST" | grep -qw "$id"; then
    echo "skip $id: not in ALLOWLIST"; continue
  fi
  machine=$(echo "$machines" | jq -c --arg id "$id" \
    '.machines[] | select((.id|tostring) == $id)')
  if ! echo "$machine" | jq -e '.listed == true' >/dev/null; then
    echo "skip $id: not listed (this loop never republishes unlisted machines)"; continue
  fi
  price=$(echo "$machine" | jq -r '.listed_gpu_cost // empty')
  end=$(echo "$machine" | jq -r '.end_date // empty')
  chunk=$(echo "$machine" | jq -r '.listed_min_gpu_count // empty')
  disk=$(echo "$machine" | jq -r '.listed_storage_cost // empty')
  dur=$(echo "$machine" | jq -r '.duration // empty')
  if [ -z "$price" ] || [ -z "$chunk" ] || [ -z "$disk" ]; then
    echo "skip $id: missing listed_gpu_cost/listed_min_gpu_count/listed_storage_cost"; continue
  fi
  if [ -z "$dur" ]; then
    if [ -z "$end" ]; then
      echo "skip $id: missing end_date and duration"; continue
    fi
    now=$(date -u +%s)
    if ! jq -e -n --argjson end "$end" --argjson now "$now" '$end > $now' >/dev/null 2>&1; then
      echo "skip machine_id=$id: end_date expired"; continue
    fi
  fi
  hidden=false
  if echo "$HIDDEN_IDS" | grep -qw "$id"; then hidden=true; fi
  # Opt-in only: skip machines with no positive reliability commitment (do not invent 0.99)
  if ! echo "$machine" | jq -e '(.sla_reliability_claim // 0) > 0' >/dev/null; then
    echo "skip $id: no SLA terms visible (SLA off, daemon below SLA minimum, or account not SLA-approved)"; continue
  fi
  # sla_sigma_x is omitted on purpose: create_asks keeps each machine's current confidence.
  # price_disk is required: null or omitted resets storage price to 0.10.
  # Send duration OR end_date, never both (400). duration must be an integer;
  # show machines returns a float, which the API rejects. hidden_listing must be explicit.
  body=$(echo "$machine" | jq -c --argjson hidden "$hidden" \
    '{machine: (.id|tonumber), price_gpu: .listed_gpu_cost, price_disk: .listed_storage_cost,
      min_chunk: .listed_min_gpu_count, sla_r_claim: .sla_reliability_claim,
      hidden_listing: $hidden}
     + (if .duration == null then {end_date} else {duration: (.duration | floor)} end)')
  if [ "$DRY_RUN" != "0" ]; then
    echo "dry-run machine_id=$id payload: $body"; continue
  fi
  resp=$(curl -s -X PUT "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/machines/create_asks/" \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $VAST_API_KEY" \
    -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
    -d "$body")
  ok=$(echo "$resp" | jq -r '.success // empty')
  if [ "$ok" = "true" ]; then
    echo "machine_id=$id ok"
  else
    echo "machine_id=$id error: $(echo "$resp" | jq -c '{success,error,msg}' 2>/dev/null || echo "$resp")"
  fi
  sleep 2
done
```

## Economics walkthrough

**Setup:** You list an H100 at **$3.50/hr** GPU price with reliability commitment **99%** and normal confidence (`sla_sigma_x = 0.5`). A client searches with **target 95%**.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Daily compute (`C`) | ~$84/day ($3.50 × 24 hr GPU only — simplified) |
| SLA fee (client pays) | ~**$0.008/hr** (~$0.20/day at 24 hr) |

SLA credits are capped relative to that UTC day's covered compute — GPU, storage, and bandwidth combined — not GPU compute alone. The simplified example below uses GPU-only math for readability; treat the [largest single-day credit](/host/sla-backtester#summarize-with-jq) from the SLA backtester as the authoritative tail-risk figure before you list.

SLA fee sizing uses internal SLA math. Use the [SLA backtester](/host/sla-backtester) to preview single-day credit exposure (largest single-day credit) and break-even reliability (`r_be`), not exact client SLA fees. For authoritative client-visible charges, use **Set Pricing → SLA** in the console or **`dph_total`** in search offers at a chosen `target_reliability`.

<Note>
If you omit `r_target`, the backtester sets the renter target equal to your reliability commitment (`r_claim`), which is a **conservative** what-if (as if every client targeted your reliability commitment). Pass `r_target` to model clients who choose lower targets; they usually see lower SLA fees. Use the backtester's largest single-day credit as your worst single-day credit reference, then verify visible charges in **Set Pricing → SLA** (or `dph_total` in search) before listing.
</Note>

### Search / client-visible fields

When SLA is enabled on an offer, clients see:

- **`dph_total`** — total $/hour including the SLA fee at their `target_reliability` (authoritative for client-visible all-in pricing).
- **`expected_reliability`** — your advertised reliability commitment (maps to `sla_r_claim`).
- **`reliability` / `reliability2`** — historical machine reliability scores, **not** your reliability commitment.

**Filter vs response field:** In search queries, the `reliability` filter uses the machine's historical reliability score — not the reliability commitment. The published reliability commitment appears in responses as `expected_reliability`. To select SLA-listed offers programmatically, use `has_sla=true` or filter on `expected_reliability > 0`; do not use the `reliability` filter as an SLA selector.

Verify after listing — filter the offer you care about; sliced machines often return multiple rows (different `num_gpus`, bid vs on-demand). Match disk allocation before comparing `dph_total` across channels (CLI default is often 5 GiB; console Search is typically 10 GB; REST examples often use 8 GB `allocated_storage`):

```bash
vastai search offers 'machine_id=12345 verified=any has_sla=true' --storage 10 --raw \
  | jq '[.[] | select(.is_bid == false) | {id, num_gpus, dph_total, expected_reliability, reliability}]'
```

### If reliability is strong relative to the client's target

In the **You net** column, values follow host earnings sign conventions: positive means SLA fee you kept; negative means credit you owed the client.

| Blended reliability vs the client's target | You net (per day) |
| --- | --- |
| Meets or exceeds the target (for example blended ~99% vs a 95% target) | +$0.20 (keep the full SLA fee) |

When the blended measure meets the target, you keep the SLA fee. These rows are not raw uptime.

<Note>
Settlement uses a **blended** reliability measure (recent plus longer-term uptime), not the day's raw uptime alone. A day whose raw uptime is at or slightly above the target can still settle with a small partial credit if the blended measure falls below it. When the blended measure meets the target, no credit is paid.
</Note>

### What if measured reliability is between the client's target and my reliability commitment?

Example: you publish **reliability commitment 99%**, the client chose **target 95%**, and the **blended** measure is **~97%**.

- **Credits** are evaluated against the client's **target** (95%), not your reliability commitment.
- Because that blended 97% is above 95%, you **owe no SLA credit** for that day. Raw uptime in the same range can still credit if the blend is below the target.
- **SLA fees** are priced from your **reliability commitment**, confidence, and the client's target — a higher reliability commitment relative to the target generally means lower client charges.

The **reliability commitment** is your belief and pricing knob. The client's **reliability target** is what drives service-credit eligibility.

### If the client's target is missed

<Warning>
Credits scale relative to the day's compute, not only the SLA fee collected. A single bad day near the client's target boundary can cost as much as 200+ days of collected SLA fees. Review the economics below and run the [backtester](/host/sla-backtester) before enabling SLA.
</Warning>

These scenarios show settlement when reliability **drops to or below** a 95% client target — they are boundary illustrations, not typical strong days:

| Measured reliability (simplified) | You net (per day) |
| --- | --- |
| Just below target on the blended measure (daily uptime may look close to 95%) | −$42 (credit you owe) |
| Well below target (~90% or less) | −$84 (large credit owed) |

To put the asymmetry in perspective: at ~$0.20/day in SLA fees, it takes roughly 210 strong days to accumulate the $42 you could lose on a single day at the target boundary. The SLA fee is a small fraction of daily compute, while credits scale against total daily compute.

Your maximum SLA loss on any single settlement day is bounded by that day's covered compute (GPU, storage, and bandwidth).

## Risk scenarios

### Reliability commitment 99%, delivered 99.5%

You outperform your reliability commitment. Clients who chose targets at or below 99% pay small SLA fees; credits are rare. You net positive on SLA over time.

### Reliability commitment 99% with an offline day

Clients receive credits tied to how far measured reliability falls below **their** target. Extended outages can approach a full-day compute credit. Your reliability score and verification status may also be affected separately from SLA settlement.

### Clients target near your reliability commitment

SLA fees are **much higher** when the client's target sits near your reliability commitment (little headroom). Prefer a reliability commitment you can sustain above the targets clients actually choose.

### You widen confidence (`sla_sigma_x` → 1.0)

Clients pay more because you admit greater uncertainty. Use this if your reliability history is volatile or the machine is new.

### Scheduled maintenance

<Note title="One window, one path">
Notify renters in the console ([Set a maintenance window](/host/set-maintenance-window)) or with CLI/REST. After you schedule from the CLI or API, confirm the window under **Host → Machines → Settings → Machine Maintenances**. Do not schedule the same downtime twice. Maintenance-forgiveness review looks for at least 24 hours of notice. Forgiveness is not guaranteed, and it does not pay the host.
</Note>

**Or use CLI/REST below.** Forgiveness is not guaranteed either way.

**Planned SLA maintenance (may reduce the service credits you owe — not guaranteed).** Schedule at least 24 hours in advance with [`vastai schedule maint`](/host/cli/schedule-maint) (or the [REST schedule-maintenance API](/api-reference/machines/schedule-maint)). Include `--sdate`, `--duration`, and `--maintenance_category`. The CLI always sends a category; its default `"not provided"` is rejected with `invalid_args`. Use one of `power`, `internet`, `disk`, `gpu`, `software`, or `other`. Scheduling maintenance ahead can cause part of the window to count as uptime when the SLA is settled. That reduces the service credits you may owe. It does not pay you or the renter. The longer the window, the smaller the share that is forgiven. Rescheduling resets the notice clock. Renters see maintenance status under **Instances**. Maintenance status is not a separate field on instance webhooks or `GET /api/v0/charges/`. Include `duration` (whole hours) and `maintenance_category`. The server does not currently apply `maintenance_reason`, so renters will not see a reason you send.

**`sdate` is Unix epoch seconds (UTC)** for both CLI `--sdate` and the REST body. **`--duration` is in hours.** The CLI asks `Continue? [y/n]` before it sends the request; in scripts or cron, pipe the answer (`echo y | vastai schedule maint …`) or call REST directly. If you still get HTTP 429, wait longer and retry. See [Rate Limits and Errors](/api-reference/rate-limits-and-errors).

# macOS: date -u -j -v+2d -v2H -v0M -v0S +%s

  --sdate "$(date -u -d '2 days 02:00:00' +%s)" \

  --maintenance_category software

## When things go wrong

- **Listing rejected (`invalid_sla_params`)** — Re-check the reliability commitment and `sla_sigma_x` against [validation rules](#validation-rules).
- **`sla_not_approved`** — Contact support; you can keep a non-SLA listing while approval is pending.
- **`sla_daemon_outdated`** — Update the host daemon from the [host setup guide](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/setup/), confirm the machine reconnects on **Host → Machines**, then retry LIST / `create_asks`. If the error persists, contact support with the machine ID.
- **Unexpected credit** — Review reliability; re-run the backtester over the affected dates and check `escrow_to_client_balance` and `reliability` on those days.
- **Severe outage (active SLA contracts):**
  1. Notify renters now: `echo y | vastai schedule maint MACHINE_ID --sdate "$(date -u +%s)" --duration HOURS --maintenance_category other`.
  2. Stop new SLA rentals. The fastest way is `vastai unlist machine MACHINE_ID`, which publishes nothing. Setting the reliability commitment to `0` (`sla_r_claim=0`) only works while the machine is online. If it is down, `create_asks` returns HTTP 402 and `vastai unlist machine` is the path that works. Only change the commitment on machines that are currently listed, because `create_asks` publishes a live listing. Include the current `price_disk` on that update.
  3. Fix the machine. Existing SLA contracts keep their terms and settle against each renter's target; unlisting or setting the commitment to `0` does not change that.
  4. After the next UTC midnight, check `sla_earn` for the affected day.
  5. Contact [Discord](https://discord.gg/hSuEbSQ4X8) before relisting.

## Monitoring SLA after listing

After you enable SLA, charges and credits settle on UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) day boundaries. SLA earnings rows typically appear in **Host → Billing** within a few hours after UTC midnight (exact timing can vary).

For SLA contracts, `sla_earn` is the escrow released to you that day: your net compute earnings plus the SLA fee, minus any service credits paid to the renter. It matches the backtester's `escrow_to_host_balance`. SLA-contract compute is **not** included in `gpu_earn`, `sto_earn`, or bandwidth earnings, so those fields stay near zero for SLA rentals. `sla_earn` is normally positive and is negative only when a prior day is corrected. Do not treat a smaller `sla_earn` as a negative credit, and do not compare it to the backtester's largest single-day credit.

When you poll the API, use **`per_machine[].sla_earn`** for a single machine. **`per_day[].sla_earn` is always fleet-wide**—even if you pass `machid`.

### Console (recommended for day-to-day checks)

1. Go to **Host → Billing** (or **Earnings** / **Reports**).
2. Set the date range to include the UTC day you want to review.
3. Filter by machine ID if available.
4. Look for the SLA settlement in the earnings breakdown. On an SLA rental, compute pay is released with the SLA fee (the same amount as `sla_earn` in the API), not as a separate GPU earnings line.
5. After downtime, a smaller release than a normal day can mean a service credit was paid. Do not look for a negative SLA line, and do not match that amount to the backtester's largest single-day credit.

**If you don't see SLA rows:** widen the date range (settlement uses UTC day boundaries), confirm the machine had an active on-demand rental that day, and confirm SLA was enabled and the offer was **listed** (not just saved).

<Note>
SLA settlement does not currently emit host webhooks. Monitor via console billing or poll `machine-earnings`; settlements align to UTC calendar days.
</Note>

```bash
vastai show earnings --machine_id <id> --start_date "$(date -u -v-7d +%F)T00:00:00Z" --end_date "$(date -u +%F)T00:00:00Z"
```

Keep the `T00:00:00Z` suffix: the CLI parses bare dates such as `2026-07-24` in your machine's local time, so append `T00:00:00Z` for an exact UTC day. On Windows, use WSL or the API example below. See [`show earnings`](/cli/reference/show-earnings) for all options.

**Linux (GNU date) — one UTC day (exact settlement check):**

```bash
# Replace 12345 with your machine ID.
vastai show earnings --machine_id 12345 --start_date "$(date -u -d 'yesterday' +%F)T00:00:00Z" --end_date "$(date -u -d 'yesterday' +%F)T00:00:00Z" --raw \
  | jq '.per_machine[] | select(.machine_id == 12345 or .machine_id == "12345") | {machine_id, sla_earn}'
```

Weekly aggregates are window totals, not daily series — use a one-day range above for day-level checks.

### API (automation)

<Note>
**Console users:** Skip this section if you check billing in **Host → Billing**. No API key is required.
</Note>

**API keys for SLA**

| Task | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| SLA backtester | `machine_read` |
| List or update SLA on an offer | `machine_write` |
| Post-listing SLA earnings | `billing_read` |

`GET /api/v0/users/me/machine-earnings/` returns SLA fields alongside compute earnings. The path user id is ignored; the account is the API key owner, so a **`billing_read`** key is enough (you do not need `user_read`). See [`show earnings`](/api-reference/billing/show-earnings).

This endpoint is rate-limited. Poll `GET /api/v0/users/me/machine-earnings/` at most about once every two seconds as a safe default (OpenAPI `x-rate-limit` threshold `2.0` on [Show Earnings](/api-reference/billing/show-earnings)). On HTTP 429, back off and retry per [Rate Limits and Errors](/api-reference/rate-limits-and-errors). For daily SLA settlement, one pull with `last_days=1` after UTC rollover is usually enough.

**Automation pattern:** There is no host webhook for SLA settlement. Poll `machine-earnings` and read `per_machine[].sla_earn` (the escrow released that day, not a credit balance). Call machines sequentially with backoff on 429. Do not use `per_day[].sla_earn` for a single machine.

<Warning>
When you pass `machid`, use `per_machine[].sla_earn` for that machine's SLA settlement in the window. Do **not** read `per_day[].sla_earn` for single-machine checks — `per_day[]` is always fleet-wide, even with `?machid=`.

**Quick rules:**
- **One machine, one UTC day:** Use `sday`/`eday` with `day = floor(unix_epoch_seconds / 86400)`, or CLI `--start_date` / `--end_date` with an explicit UTC time (`YYYY-MM-DDT00:00:00Z`; bare dates are parsed in local time). Example: `2026-07-24` 00:00 UTC is unix `1784851200` → `sday=eday=20658`. To cross-check the backtester, compare `sla_earn` with that day's `escrow_to_host_balance`. Never compare `sla_earn` with the largest single-day credit (`escrow_to_client_balance`). Use `last_days=1` with `machid` only for coarse polling after UTC rollover—not as a strict match to one settled UTC calendar day.
- **Fleet snapshot (coarse / recent window only):** one request without `machid` returns all machines in `per_machine[]` for the window.
</Warning>

```bash
# Exact UTC day 2026-07-24 for machine 12345 (sday/eday = 20658)
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $VAST_API_KEY" \
  "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/users/me/machine-earnings/?machid=12345&sday=20658&eday=20658" \
  | jq '.per_machine[] | select(.machine_id == 12345 or .machine_id == "12345") | {machine_id, sla_earn}'
```

```bash
# Fleet snapshot (last_days=1 — coarse, not a settled calendar day)
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $VAST_API_KEY" \
  "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/users/me/machine-earnings/?last_days=1" \
  | jq '.per_machine[] | {machine_id, sla_earn}'
```

**Machine-scoped coarse poll (`last_days=1`, not a settled calendar day):**

```bash
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $VAST_API_KEY" \
  "https://console.vast.ai/api/v0/users/me/machine-earnings/?machid=12345&last_days=1" \
  | jq '.per_machine[] | select(.machine_id == 12345 or .machine_id == "12345") | {machine_id, sla_earn}'
```

Do **not** compare `last_days=7` totals to the backtester's largest single-day credit.

- Settlement is evaluated on UTC day boundaries. Rows typically update within a few hours after UTC midnight.

## Tips for competitive SLA offers

- **Commit to a level you can sustain.** Clients who choose reliability targets below your reliability commitment see a lower SLA fee and still get a meaningful SLA.
- **Tighten confidence only when earned.** Use a tighter spread (`sla_sigma_x ≈ 0.2`) after you have a strong reliability track record on that machine.
- **Keep the machine online during active contracts.** SLA credits and verification are separate systems, but both respond to downtime.
- **SLA applies to on-demand rentals only.** It does not apply to interruptible/bid instances or reserved/prepay rentals. When a client starts an on-demand rental on an SLA-enabled offer, SLA terms lock for that rental contract and do not change if you later edit the offer.
- **Use the [SLA backtester](/host/sla-backtester) (API-only)** to preview how different reliability commitments and confidence levels would have affected past earnings.
